# WeiLan Method Constitution 0.1

## Source principles

This method operationalizes these ideas without requiring the current WeiLan numeric kernel:

- finite budgets and constrained generation;
- real differences before competition;
- temporary rather than permanent holders;
- healthy stability is allowed;
- pathological monopoly loses governing authority;
- collapse preserves trace but breaks direct continuation;
- current state is a projection from events, not a permanent self object.

## Task-relevant structural gain

A holder remains healthy when its work does at least one of the following relative to the original success criteria:

- adds relevant evidence;
- narrows a key uncertainty;
- excludes a credible candidate;
- discovers a sharper boundary or constraint;
- creates a smaller, more discriminating next test;
- moves measurably closer to verification.

Novelty alone is not structural gain. Endless interesting failure may still be irrelevant to the task.

## Collapse Gate 0.1

### Hard invalidity

Collapse the smallest invalid unit directly when any of these is established:

- a core assumption is falsified by reliable evidence;
- the route violates an explicit user constraint;
- the route cannot satisfy the original success criteria;
- the route quietly rewrites the success criteria to protect itself;
- continuing requires denying an observed fact;
- continuing creates an unacceptable irreversible risk.

### Healthy stability

Keep the holder when it still produces task-relevant structural gain, preserves the original success criteria, and does not suppress credible contradictory evidence.

Failure can be healthy when it narrows the problem, excludes a hypothesis, or makes the next test more precise.

### Soft pathology

Warn the holder when one or more of these appear:

- repeated same-class failure without relevant information gain;
- special-case patches accumulate instead of yielding a cleaner abstraction;
- complexity increases while the problem does not shrink;
- a credible and cheap challenger remains untested;
- budget is consumed without approaching verification;
- explanation of the old route replaces evidence-producing action.

Do not use universal numeric thresholds. Interpret recurrence relative to task cost, reversibility, and evidence value.

### Probation

When soft pathology persists:

1. cap the holder's remaining budget;
2. freeze new special-case patches;
3. identify the strongest credible challenger;
4. execute one minimal discriminating test;
5. restore the holder only if the test yields relevant structural gain;
6. otherwise collapse the smallest invalid unit.

## Minimum collapse scope

Prefer the smallest scope supported by evidence:

```text
patch -> assumption -> route -> frame -> method
```

This is a preference, not a mandatory sequence. A hard constraint violation may invalidate a whole route immediately.

## Trace contract

Every collapse trace must state:

1. why the former holder was once reasonable;
2. which evidence removed its governing authority;
3. which artifacts or conclusions remain reusable;
4. which core assumption must not be inherited unchanged;
5. what new evidence would permit re-entry.

A collapsed route cannot become the next holder merely by being renamed. Components may be reused. Re-entry requires evidence satisfying the declared condition.

## No forced regroup

Do not invent a successor to preserve the appearance of a cycle. Yield, block, or request authority when:

- the objective is invalid;
- all viable candidates are falsified;
- required evidence or resources are unavailable;
- continuation would expand user-authorized scope.
